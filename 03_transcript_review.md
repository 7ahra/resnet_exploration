## Answer-first restructures (reasoning paths — speak your own version, then bring it back for grading)

**#1 Proposition.** S1 = the definition itself: a declarative statement that can be held true or false — the content slot after "I believe that…" (already in your §3 via Rokeach — [CHECK] exact wording before quoting him by name). S2 = its role: criterion 1 of the four-part rule; the other three test HOW the proposition is held. S3 = one pass/fail pair from doctoral education. What happened instead: you navigated slides, described the criteria in general, gave the chair example — and never defined the term. Jordan's debrief handed you the answer: "a statement, a declaration."

**#2 Belief vs opinion.** The first word ("No") was right; everything after collapsed, and the session closed on "I'll answer all of your questions in a monthly." Skeleton: S1 = "No — a belief differs from an opinion in that [your committed contrast]." Then run one candidate through your own four criteria showing which cells an opinion fails. Knowledge: the boundary already in your paper (a knowledge claim answers to evidence — Green). Behavior: the observable stream beliefs are inferred from (Pajares). **Opinion is defined nowhere in your paper — you must commit to an operationalization you own** (e.g., held true but lacking the evaluative-guide and resistance profile) and present it as the study's operationalization — or verify a source first. Never improvise an attribution. Full drill: 05/D1.

**#3 Four criteria with examples.** The ask was a 4×2 grid: per criterion, one passer and one near-miss failing exactly that criterion. You built one wandering example (mostly "personal") and invented off-codebook criteria mid-answer ("answering about what questions, not like why and how questions" — that's interview design, not the decision rule; and at 15:30, "experience or story on the back" — that's Nespor's episodic-grounding *feature*, not one of your four criteria — label it as such if you use it, [CHECK] Nespor first). Skeleton: criterion → passing statement → failing statement → which cell it fails → next. Four rounds, stop. Jordan's chart suggestion is this drill in slide form.

**#4 Personal criterion.** You overclaimed ("we never sit and write something about. **#5 Reform implications.** The clarifying instinct is good — deploy it BEFORE the detour, not after. The answer was on your closing slide the whole time.
