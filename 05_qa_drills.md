# 05 — Q&A Drill Bank

**How to use this file.** These are reasoning scaffolds, not scripts. For each drill: read the scaffold once, close the file, speak your answer aloud, record it, then grade sentence 1 against the template below. If it sounds memorized in mocks, I will change the angle — the goal is reasoning you own. The committee circles back to earlier questions; drill in shuffled order and re-ask yourself earlier drills at the end of every session.

**The answer-first template (every question, every time):**
1. **Position** — one sentence that stands alone as the answer if you're cut off.
2. **Evidence** — participant, quote, count, or source.
3. **Boundary** — what the claim does not cover.
4. **Stop.**
If the question is ambiguous, clarify BEFORE answering — never after a detour. Banned utterances: "I forgot," "whatever," "I see" (as the whole answer), "I'll answer later." When you genuinely don't know: "I don't know; here's how I'd find out" — only when true.

**Exemplar of the pattern (from attempt-1 feedback):** *"What does 'empirical' mean?"* → "Empirical means claims were linked with collected data; 90% of the scholarly articles did not explicitly link their claims to any data." Answer, then support. Nothing before the answer.
